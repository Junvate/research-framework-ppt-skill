# Style System

## Visual Character

Use a clean scientific and engineering presentation style:

- white canvas with strong black titles;
- a restrained blue divider under the title;
- blue for inputs, core mechanisms, or primary processes;
- orange for construction, transition, emphasis, or feedback;
- green for outputs, validation, or accepted results;
- neutral gray for supporting data, tools, constraints, and baselines.

Keep enough black and neutral space so the slide does not become a single-color interface.

## Typography

For a 16:9 slide near 1280 by 720 pixels, use these starting sizes:

- slide title: 40 to 46 pixels;
- purpose line: 24 to 29 pixels;
- section heading: 21 to 25 pixels;
- module title: 16 to 20 pixels;
- supporting detail: 14 to 17 pixels.

Use one Chinese-capable sans-serif family throughout unless the supplied template defines another type system. Keep letter spacing at zero.

## Geometry

- Prefer a flat composition with a few large regions over many floating cards.
- Use sharp or slightly rounded rectangles with a radius no greater than 8 pixels.
- Keep repeated modules equal in size and align them to stable grid tracks.
- Leave a visible gap between connectors and text.
- Keep outer margins near 30 to 60 pixels and reserve the bottom strip for constraints only when needed.

## Connectors

- Use straight connectors for simple sequences and elbow connectors for dependencies.
- Keep a consistent left-to-right or top-to-bottom reading direction.
- Use one arrowhead at the destination.
- Aggregate parallel inputs or outputs before the next major stage.
- Place feedback loops outside the primary reading path.
- Do not let connectors cross labels, module text, or unrelated boxes.

## Content Density

Technical framework slides may be dense, but every text group must remain readable at presentation size. Reduce wording before reducing font size. Split the framework into two slides when the reader must understand two different structural grammars or when more than roughly five major stages compete for attention.
